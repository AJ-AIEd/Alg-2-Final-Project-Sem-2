Algebra 2 Cumulative Semester Final Project

The Mathematics of Food Systems

“All models are wrong, but some are useful.” George Box

Use Algebra 2 to investigate how real food systems evolve under pressure: price, transportation, labor, climate, storage, exports, infrastructure, supply, demand, and waste.

Project Philosophy

A model is useful, but incomplete.

This project is about increasingly thoughtful mathematical reasoning. Perfect prediction is not the goal. Revision, critique, and clearer assumptions are evidence that your understanding is becoming more sophisticated.

Mathematics Helps Us

Model change, identify patterns, test assumptions, compare possibilities, estimate outcomes, critique predictions, and reveal constraints.

Strong Thinkers Ask

What assumptions are we making? What variables matter most? Where does the model stop making sense? What tradeoffs appear?

Revision Counts

Changing a model is not failure. It shows you are testing the relationship between graph, algebra, context, and reality.

Strong Focus Test

A strong investigation usually includes one main changing quantity, one measurable relationship, one prediction or threshold question, one physical or cost constraint, and one clear connection across mathematical lenses.

Class Roadmap

Five phases, one investigation

Roadmap = conceptual flow. Open a phase when you need the next modeling move.

Class 1

Entering the System

What relationships are we beginning to notice?

Conceptual move: Notice relationships before choosing equations.

Modeling question: Which variables seem connected, and which are hard to measure?

Thinking shift: From topic choice to a measurable system relationship.

Focus: Observation, variables, systems thinking, uncertainty.

Vlog: Discuss patterns, unclear ideas, assumptions, and important variables.

Class 2

Modeling Change

How is the system changing over time?

Conceptual move: Represent repeated change with terms, formulas, and accumulated totals.

Modeling question: Is change happening in steps, continuously, additively, or multiplicatively?

Thinking shift: From noticing change to predicting and testing reliability.

Focus: Sequences, series, prediction, recalculation.

Vlog: Explain why the model fits and where it begins to fail.

Class 3

Acceleration, Decay, and Thresholds

When does repeated change become accelerated or compounding?

Conceptual move: Model compounding, decay, and threshold moments.

Modeling question: What changes when growth is treated as continuous rather than sampled in steps?

Thinking shift: From prediction to threshold interpretation and model breakdown.

Focus: Exponential models, logarithmic solving, realism.

Vlog: Explain what the graph reveals and when exponential behavior stops making sense.

Class 4

Constraints, Capacity, and Cost

How do physical and economic constraints reshape the system?

Conceptual move: Use structure and graph behavior to understand constraints.

Modeling question: Which parts of the polynomial are physically meaningful, and which must be rejected?

Thinking shift: From “bigger is better” to tradeoffs, extrema, and restricted domains.

Focus: Polynomial structure, capacity, cost, physical interpretation.

Vlog: Explain how the cubic structure reveals constraints and impossible regions.

Class 5

Revision, Synthesis, and Communication

What story does our mathematics tell?

Conceptual move: Turn separate models into one mathematical claim.

Modeling question: What did each model reveal, and where did each stop being useful?

Thinking shift: From completed work to a defensible explanation of uncertainty.

Focus: Synthesis, revision, interpretation, limitations.

Vlog: Discuss how understanding evolved and what limitations remain.

Mathematical Lenses

Different structures reveal different parts of the system.

Use each lens to make a different kind of mathematical meaning. The goal is not to complete units in order; the goal is to understand the system more clearly.

Sequences & Series

Big question: How is the system changing over time?

Use this lens for repeated change, terms, totals, prediction, recalculation, and cumulative effects.

Exponentials & Logarithms

Big question: When does repeated change become accelerated, compounding, decaying, or threshold-based?

Use this lens for growth factors, thresholds, logarithmic solving, and model breakdown.

Polynomials

Big question: How do physical and economic constraints reshape the system?

Use this lens for structure, capacity, cost, extrema, end behavior, and realistic domains.

Required Mathematical Comparisons

Use comparisons only when they clarify the model.

Comparisons should reveal representation, structure, realism, interpretation, assumptions, or constraints.

Class 2 — Sequences & SeriesDiscrete change and accumulated change
  • Discrete vs continuous: Are values measured in steps, or can they change between measurements?
  • Arithmetic vs geometric: Is change added or multiplied each step?
  • Recursive vs explicit: Do you need the previous term, or can you jump directly to any term?
  • Term vs total: Does the model describe one moment or accumulated change?
Purpose: decide whether a sequence is the most honest representation of the system.
Class 3 — Exponentials & LogsGrowth, thresholds, and representation
  • Linear vs exponential: Is change constant, or does it depend on the current amount?
  • Sampled vs continuous: How does modeling continuously change the meaning compared to yearly terms?
  • Table vs graph: What does the graph reveal that the sequence table does not?
  • Thresholds: Why are logarithms needed to solve for an unknown time?
  • Realism: When does continuous exponential behavior stop making sense?
Purpose: connect representation choice to threshold meaning and prediction limits.
Class 4 — PolynomialsStructure, behavior, and physical constraints
  • Cubic structure: What does multiplying interacting dimensions reveal?
  • Factored vs standard form: Which form makes zeros, dimensions, or comparison easier to see?
  • Odd vs even degree: How would the graph behave differently if the degree changed?
  • Leading coefficient: Why does one side rise while the other falls?
  • End behavior: Interpret as x → ∞ and as x → −∞ in context.
  • Physical meaning: What do multiplicity, extrema, and restricted domains suggest?
Purpose: use graph structure to identify capacity, tradeoffs, and impossible regions.

Lightweight Model Fit Check

Correlation, r, and R²: How strong is the relationship?

This is a credibility and critique tool, not a full statistics unit. The purpose is interpretation, not manual calculation.

What r and R² mean

r: Direction and Strength

  • r is the correlation coefficient.
  • It is between −1 and 1.
  • r close to 1: strong positive relationship.
  • r close to −1: strong negative relationship.
  • r close to 0: little or no linear relationship.

R²: Model Fit

  • R² is the coefficient of determination.
  • It tells how much variation is explained by the model.
  • R² = 0.85 means about 85% explained.
  • A higher R² can mean a better fit, but it does not prove truth or causation.

Quick Interpretations

  • r = 0.92: strong and positive.
  • r = −0.78: fairly strong and negative.
  • R² = 0.64: about 64% explained, 36% unexplained.
Student task and AI critique prompt

Student Task

  1. Choose two variables from your investigation.
  2. Create a scatterplot and add or estimate a trendline/model.
  3. Use AI to help explain r and R² in context.
  4. If possible, verify r and R² with Desmos, Google Sheets, a graphing calculator, or another reliable tool.
  5. If you cannot verify it, state that limitation honestly.
  6. Interpret what r and/or R² suggests about model credibility and limitations.

Required AI Prompt Box

Copy and paste your two-variable data or describe your scatterplot. Ask AI: What does r suggest about direction and strength? What does R² suggest about model fit? What limitations should we be careful about? What hidden variables might affect this relationship? Does this imply causation? Why or why not?

Then critique: What did AI help us understand? What still needs verification? What limitations remain? How does this affect the credibility of our claim?

Correlation ≠ causation: This check supports model credibility and limitations. It should not become a major assessed skill or replace mathematical interpretation.

Rubrics

The rubric separates content standards from skill grades.

The project assesses reasoning, interpretation, critique, revision, contextual understanding, visible thinking, and algebraic precision. It does not reward production polish as mathematical achievement.

Standards 1–3

Core Algebra 2 Content

Sequences and series, exponentials and logarithms, and polynomials are assessed as mathematical content standards.

Standards 4–6

Cross-Cutting Skill Grades

Communication, model comparison, limitations, revision, algebraic precision, and flexible number sense are assessed across the whole investigation.

1. Sequences and Series

Select and justify sequence and series models. Distinguish arithmetic vs geometric, recursive vs explicit, finite vs infinite, and discrete vs continuous representations when relevant. Explain terms, parameters, cumulative sums, predictions, recalculations, and interpretations in context.

2. Exponentials and Logarithms

Select and justify exponential models. Explain growth and decay, thresholds, growth factors, and logarithmic reasoning in context. Compare exponential behavior to linear or geometric behavior when appropriate and critique limitations realistically.

3. Polynomials

Create and analyze polynomial models. Explain degree, leading coefficient, end behavior, intercepts, multiplicity, extrema, realistic domain restrictions, factored form, standard form, and notation such as x → ∞ and x → −∞ in context.

4. Mathematical Communication and Interpretation Skill Grade

Communicate mathematical thinking clearly through explanations, graphs, annotations, tables, discussion, and contextual interpretation. Make connections among graph, algebra, assumptions, and context visible.

5. Comparing Models, Assumptions, and Limitations Skill Grade

Compare multiple models, assumptions, and representations. Critique realism, hidden variables, tradeoffs, assumptions, unreasonable predictions, and show revision that strengthens the investigation.

6. Algebraic Precision and Flexible Number Sense Skill Grade

Solve accurately using appropriate algebraic methods such as factoring, logarithms, inverse operations, substitution, graph interpretation, and algebraic manipulation. Use rational numbers, percentages, negative values, and units precisely.
